# Mochi & Chonk: diverse actions and costume research

Date: 8 October 2026. Output: **24 new action concepts across six transparent sheets**, twelve per character. [Browse the gallery](output/characters/diverse-activities/index.html).

## Brief and approach

The user wants recognizable Mochi and Chonk doing genuinely different things, including wearing clothes, with animation to follow. Earlier neutral/cheer sheets establish the identity; they should not constrain every future illustration to a seated hamster holding a new object.

This study combines natural hamster behavior, published character/sticker activity ranges, costume variations and animation staging. Sources inform movement and situations; the newly generated art uses only the user-approved Mochi or Chonk sheet as its image reference. Other mascots were not supplied to the generator. Every sheet references the same approved model directly, avoiding a chain of increasingly altered generations.

### Research limits

Official behavior and animation pages were read as textual sources. The earlier LycheeGelly study included direct visual inspection and one downloaded source image. Marketplace/search image previews helped identify activity categories; they are not a claim that every product image was inspected in detail. The Etsy lifestyle-pack link returned an unavailable-item page, and a direct marketplace preview-image fetch failed; neither was used as a visual identity reference. This is a broad focused study, not a scientific ranking of cuteness or an exhaustive inventory of hamster art.

## Character identity contract

| Feature | Mochi | Chonk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main color | Warm gray/taupe | Golden brown/tan |
| Lower face/body | Cream muzzle and belly | Large cream muzzle and belly |
| Cheeks | Soft pink | Warm peach |
| Body | Smaller, soft low bean; can sit upright for an action | Larger, heavier and rounder; grounded coach presence |
| Face | Tiny pink nose, simple dot/arc eyes, short curved mouth | Same simple facial vocabulary with his own approved spacing |
| Anatomy | Tiny rounded ears, very short paws, small feet | Tiny rounded ears and short paws against a substantial belly |
| Rendering | Warm dark-brown irregular outline; soft painted texture | Same outline and texture language |

Do not introduce long human arms/legs, fingers, a slim waist, a long snout, a cat tail, eyelashes or glossy oversized eyes. Body position can change; species and personality must remain apparent. When paired, Mochi must still read smaller than Chonk. Individual sheets fill their available space, so they are **not** a cross-character scale reference.

### Clothing rules

- Leave the face visible and keep recognizable ears/gray or brown fur showing. Hats sit between/behind ears; raincoat and hoodie hoods stay down.
- Fit clothes around the round body. Sleeves end at tiny paws; clothing does not imply a new human skeleton.
- Let one main outfit communicate the role: apron, raincoat, pajamas, cardigan, overalls or cape. Avoid a pile of unrelated accessories.
- Use a restrained palette that complements the fur: lilac, peach, mint and butter yellow for Mochi; sage, rust, muted denim and oatmeal for Chonk. These are defaults, not locked gender rules.
- Keep accessories separable in later production: hats, scarf ends, apron ties, rope, cape and props should become their own layers.
- Costumes and exercises are fictional mascot scenes, not real-hamster care instructions or human exercise-form demonstrations.

## Review and animation handoff

All six sheets were visually inspected after generation. The character colors, cream faces, blush, small ears and painted line treatment remain recognizable across the new situations. Outfit detail varies, and faces are not pixel-identical to the model sheet. Approve/clean up the selected pose before building a complete motion set around it.

Each saved PNG is 1536×1024 with 32-bit RGBA data; the sampled corner has alpha zero. This checks file dimensions and the presence of transparency, not every edge pixel. The gallery composites the images on a pale surface so alpha can be viewed correctly. No production character files were replaced.

Before animation:

1. Pick one concept and make neutral, anticipation, action and settle drawings **within that same outfit and viewpoint**. The four unrelated activities on a sheet must not be cycled as if they were consecutive frames.
2. Prepare body clean plates, alternate eyes/mouth, visible paws, clothes and props as separate parts. These generated sheets are flattened images, not layered rigs.
3. Register the body/ground anchor and control the size. Reuse a stable face/body wherever possible instead of accepting texture and outline shimmer between generated frames.
4. Check identity at 80–160 px. Simplify knitting stitches, tiny book details, flour and telescope trim if they interfere with the character at that size.
5. Animate the main action first. Add at most one secondary movement initially: scarf tail, cape, umbrella, rope or cheek pulse. Preserve visible paw/prop contact.
6. Test event behavior, reduced motion and mobile rendering using the existing [animation plan](ANIMATION_PLAN.md).

Suggested first production choices: M09 coin drop for savings; C03 coaching nod for exercise readiness; M04 recovery breath for rest; M05 stirring for a playful reward; M10 dancing and C11 parade for larger milestones. Rope skipping and knitting are more complex because small contacts and overlapping props must remain consistent.

The [generation manifest](output/characters/diverse-activities/generation-manifest.json) preserves the exact prompts and identity-reference paths. The six versioned PNGs are the deliverables; the gallery and catalog provide selection and handoff context.
